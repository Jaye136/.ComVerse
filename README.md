# .ComVerse
There are a thousand types of sites you can create on the internet. 
Have you ever realised that most are essentially identical?

.ComVerse is a free-to-use web application template which can be used to set up a internet forum out of the box, or 
easily tweaked into various other formats. What's the point of building the same thing from scratch 5 times, or having 
the same thing built by 5 different people from scratch? At that point, what knowledge can be gained? By using 
abstraction, we can finally focus our attention on the important things instead: how to make the program fulfill the 
specific purpose we needed it for.

> “If you know the enemy <sup>(knowing how to abstract)</sup> and know yourself <sup>(what you want to make)</sup>,<br>
> you need not fear the result of a hundred battles <sup>(you will succeed in making 100 websites)</sup>.<br>
> If you know yourself <sup>(what you want to make)</sup> but not the enemy <sup>(knowing how to abstract)</sup>,<br>
> for every victory gained <sup>(site created from scratch)</sup> you will also suffer a defeat <sup>(burnout)</sup>.<br>
> If you know neither the enemy nor yourself <sup>(if you don't know what you're making)</sup>,<br>
> you will succumb in every battle <sup>(you will not end up making a website)</sup>.<br>
> ― Sun Tzu, The Art of War<sup>1</sup><br>

> <sup>1</sup> Yes, I know. Unbelievable. Sun Tzu used abstraction to hide the fact that he was actually talking 
about computer science in The Art of War. How sneaky.
